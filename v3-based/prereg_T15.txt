T15 — closing the sedenion pixel
Registered by Claude (Fable 5.1), 27 Sep 2026, before any code for T15 runs. Pedro's instruction: "close the sedenion,
otherwise you can't test, division will silently cost." Every choice below is Claude's [C].

GOAL [C]
The weight pixel of sedenion.py is 16 weights on the 16 anchors, and inference reads band 0 of conj(w)·x. The
sedenions are not a division algebra: the norm is not multiplicative (defect.py: the defect lives in the inner×outer
block, rank 8) and there are 42 zero-divisor pairs (the paper, Capability 6). So a move by a mixed pixel has no exact
inverse: x cannot be rewound from conj(w)·x, and the loss is silent. T6 closed the octonions by finding the sets the
product stays inside, with a table. T15 does the same for the pixel: find the closed unit of the sedenion move, so
that every move rewinds exactly and the norm is kept, and state what closing changes for inference.

ARITHMETIC
Exact integers only (i128 in std Rust, walk/src/bin/sed.rs). The product is the kernel's: Cayley–Dickson doubling,
SIGN[a][b] with landing a xor b, taken from legos/kernel.py's construction; the Rust table is checked entry by entry
against the recursive product and its sha256 is printed beside Python's for cross-check. Random inputs come from a
seeded xorshift; the seed is in the file.

PREDICTIONS (each can fail; failures are reported as failures)
P1 [C] The 32 signed anchors ±e_a close under the product (a loop of 32), and anchor moves rewind: for every anchor a
    and every integer sedenion x (1,000 random), e_a(e_a x) = -x and (x e_a) e_a = -x. Kill: any anchor, any x.
P2 [C] The octet is the closed unit. For w supported on the inner octet only (e0..e7), or the outer octet only
    (e8..e15), and any integer sedenion x: w(conj(w) x) = |w|² x exactly, and |w x|² = |w|² |x|² when x is on the same
    octet. 1,000 random w per octet, 1,000 random x. Kill: any failure.
P3 [C] A mixed pixel does not close: for w with support on both octets (1,000 random), w(conj(w) x) ≠ |w|² x for
    almost all x, and the defect |w|²|x|² − |w x|² is nonzero. Prediction: failure on ≥ 99% of (w, x). Held either way
    it is a measurement; the kill is P3 holding at 0%, which would mean the defect was never there.
P4 [C] Real pixels. Layer-0 q_proj of Qwen3-1.7B (local u16), the first 4,096 pixels of 16 weights, each pixel as
    integers on its own lowest band. Whole-pixel rewind w(conj(w) x) = |w|² x fails on ≥ 99% of pixels (10 random x
    each); inner-octet and outer-octet rewind hold on 100% of pixels. Kill: any octet failure.
P5 [C] Band 0 is unchanged by closing: band 0 of conj(w)·x equals band 0 of conj(w_in)·x plus band 0 of conj(w_out)·x
    on every real pixel and every x (it is the dot product, bilinear). So inference reads the same number; what
    closing removes is the 15 other bands' inner×outer cross terms, which carried the defect.
P6 Reported, no prediction: the 42 zero pairs of the reading (inner imaginary o with outer imaginary s, s ≠ o + 8)
    recomputed from the table; and the count of the 336 zero products (e_a ± e_b)(e_c ± e_d) = 0.

WHAT CLOSING MEANS FOR THE RUNTIME [C, to be confirmed by P2–P5]
The pixel is an octet, not a sedenion: 8 weights on 8 anchors, an octonion, a division algebra with exact rewind.
Two octets are two pixels, never mixed in one move. 8 octets is the same read one level up (the log's next-build
item 4). geo.rs already lands by octets; T15 says why that is the closed shape and not a convenience.
